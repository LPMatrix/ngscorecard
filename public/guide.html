Glossary & Methodology

What every status label and budget term on NGScorecard means, and how ratings, sourcing, and corrections actually work.

Promise ratings Status terms Budget terms Other terms Sourcing & corrections

1 Promise ratings

Every tracked promise — and every entry in Inherited Fixes — gets one of four ratings, based on the evidence linked to it.

Kept
The promise was fulfilled, based on verifiable evidence — an official report, government data, or credible reporting.
Partial
Meaningful progress was made, but delivery fell short of what was promised, or only part of it was carried out.
Broken
No meaningful action was taken, the promise was explicitly abandoned, or the outcome contradicts what was promised.
In progress
It's too early to assess — the stated timeline for delivery hasn't passed, or the process is still underway. Shown as "Pending" in raw data.

2 Status terms by category

Every other category has its own status vocabulary, since "kept vs. broken" doesn't fit a court case or an appointment.

Fraud cases
Convicted
A court has returned a guilty verdict.
Ongoing
Investigation or prosecution is still in progress.
Dismissed
Charges were dropped or the case was thrown out.
Acquitted
A court found the accused not guilty.

Each fraud case also carries a separate government response rating — Pursuing, Stalled, Politicised, Abandoned, or No interference — describing how the government itself has handled the allegation, independent of the case's legal outcome.

Executive orders
Implemented
The order has been executed and had a real, observable effect.
Partial
The order or its enforcement is incomplete.
Reversed
Overturned by the same administration, a later one, or a court.
Ignored
Issued but not enforced.
Ministers & commissioners
Good / Fair / Poor
A qualitative read on delivery against the ministry's stated mandate, based on reported outcomes.
Sacked
Removed from office by the president or governor.
Resigned
Left the role voluntarily.
Bills
Passed
Signed into law.
Partial
Passed one chamber, heavily amended, or stalled after passing.
Pending
Still moving through the legislative process.
Abandoned
Withdrawn, or lapsed without further action.
Appointments
Serving
Currently in the role.
Resigned
Left voluntarily.
Sacked
Removed from the role.
Court judgments
Govt Won / Govt Lost
The outcome of the ruling, from the government's side.
Settled
Resolved outside a verdict — e.g. a negotiated settlement.
Ongoing
Still before the court.

3 Budget terms

Where a figure isn't publicly available, the field is left blank rather than estimated — see Sourcing & corrections.

Total budget
The full amount appropriated for the year.
Revenue (projected)
The income the budget assumes will come in, set when the budget was passed.
Revenue (actual)
What was actually collected, once officially reported.
Deficit
The gap between spending and revenue, typically covered by borrowing.
Debt service
The portion of the budget spent servicing existing debt, before anything else is funded.
Implementation rate
The share of the appropriated budget actually spent or released.
Allocation vs. Released
Per ministry: Allocation is what was budgeted to it; Released is what was actually disbursed.

5 Sourcing & corrections

Every entry on NGScorecard — a promise, a fraud case, a budget line, an order — is dated and linked to its original source: a government release, a court filing, or reporting from an established outlet. Nothing is rated without a linked reference.

On numbers specifically: NGScorecard doesn't publish estimates dressed up as facts. Where an official figure hasn't actually been published — an unreleased state budget line, for instance — that field is left blank rather than filled in with a computed guess.

Ratings are a judgment call made against the linked evidence, not an automated calculation. If you think an entry is wrong, outdated, or missing context, email mubaraqsanusi908@gmail.com — corrections are made promptly and noted on the entry.
