1 What counts as a trackable promise

The same test is applied to every president and governor, so the counts on one scorecard mean the same thing as the counts on another.

Included
A specific, public commitment the officeholder or their campaign made before or at the start of the term — from a manifesto, the inauguration address, an official agenda document (the Renewed Hope Agenda, the Seven-Point Agenda, and so on), a budget speech, or a pledge widely reported at the time. It has to be concrete enough to check: a target, a project, a named reform.
Tracked separately
Problems the administration did not create but publicly took ownership of appear under Inherited Fixes — rated on the same four-point scale, but kept apart from campaign promises.
Excluded
Aspirational rhetoric with nothing measurable in it; one-off remarks that were never part of a documented platform; commitments made by party figures who did not hold the office; and statements made after the fact to describe policy that already existed.
Selection within a term
We track the most prominent and consequential promises across a fixed set of areas — economy, security, governance, infrastructure, power, health, education, and rights. Where an agenda has official pillars, we track against those pillars rather than picking a favourable subset.

A promise being hard to assess is not a reason to leave it out — it's a reason to rate it In progress and keep checking.

2 Promise ratings

Every tracked promise — and every entry in Inherited Fixes — gets one of four ratings, based on the evidence linked to it.

Kept
The promised thing actually happened, at roughly the promised scale, confirmed by an official record or by independent reporting that agrees. An announcement or a launch is not "kept" — delivery is.
Partial
There is documented progress — money released, a project under way, a bill introduced, a measurable but incomplete improvement — that falls short of the promise's scale, scope, or timeline.
Broken
The term, or the promise's own deadline, has passed with no meaningful action; or the promise was explicitly abandoned; or the measured outcome moved in the opposite direction.
In progress
The stated timeline hasn't passed and work is genuinely under way. Used sparingly and revisited every review cycle — it is not a permanent resting place. Shown as "Pending" in raw data.

When sources disagree, the more independent and more recent one is preferred, and the disagreement is noted in the assessment. Absence of evidence is not treated as failure: if nothing is verifiable either way and the deadline hasn't passed, the rating stays In progress; if the deadline has passed with no sign of action, that is Broken.

3 Status terms by category

Every other category has its own status vocabulary, since "kept vs. broken" doesn't fit a court case or an appointment.

Fraud cases
Convicted
A court has returned a guilty verdict.
Ongoing
Investigation or prosecution is still in progress.
Dismissed
Charges were dropped or the case was thrown out.
Acquitted
A court found the accused not guilty.

Each fraud case also carries a separate government response rating — Pursuing, Stalled, Politicised, Abandoned, or No interference — describing how the government itself has handled the allegation, independent of the case's legal outcome.

Executive orders
Implemented
The order has been executed and had a real, observable effect.
Partial
The order or its enforcement is incomplete.
Reversed
Overturned by the same administration, a later one, or a court.
Ignored
Issued but not enforced.
Ministers & commissioners
Good / Fair / Poor
A qualitative read on delivery against the ministry's stated mandate, based on reported outcomes.
Sacked
Removed from office by the president or governor.
Resigned
Left the role voluntarily.
Bills
Passed
Signed into law.
Partial
Passed one chamber, heavily amended, or stalled after passing.
Pending
Still moving through the legislative process.
Abandoned
Withdrawn, or lapsed without further action.
Appointments
Serving
Currently in the role.
Resigned
Left voluntarily.
Sacked
Removed from the role.
Court judgments
Govt Won / Govt Lost
The outcome of the ruling, from the government's side.
Settled
Resolved outside a verdict — e.g. a negotiated settlement.
Ongoing
Still before the court.

4 Budget terms

Where a figure isn't publicly available, the field is left blank rather than estimated — see Sourcing & review.

Total budget
The full amount appropriated for the year.
Revenue (projected)
The income the budget assumes will come in, set when the budget was passed.
Revenue (actual)
What was actually collected, once officially reported.
Deficit
The gap between spending and revenue, typically covered by borrowing.
Debt service
The portion of the budget spent servicing existing debt, before anything else is funded.
Implementation rate
The share of the appropriated budget actually spent or released.
Allocation vs. Released
Per ministry: Allocation is what was budgeted to it; Released is what was actually disbursed.

6 Sourcing & review

Every entry — a promise, a fraud case, a budget line, an order — is dated and linked to its original source. Nothing is rated without a linked reference, and ratings are a judgment call made against that evidence, not an automated calculation.

What counts as a source
Primary & official records
Government gazettes and budget documents, data from the NBS, CBN and DMO, court filings and judgments, official agency reports. The strongest basis for a rating.
Established independent reporting
Nigerian and international outlets with editorial standards and a public corrections process.
Named expert & civil-society research
Analysis from bodies such as BudgIT or transparency organisations — used for context and to cross-check the records above.
Not used on its own
Anonymous social media, partisan press releases, and government or opposition claims that can't be independently checked. A government's own claim may be quoted, but is labelled as a claim, not as confirmation.
On numbers

NGScorecard doesn't publish estimates dressed up as facts. Where an official figure hasn't been published — an unreleased state budget line, for instance — the field is left blank rather than filled with a computed guess. Blank means "not published," not "zero."

Review cadence

Each administration carries a reviewed date: the last time every entry under it was re-checked against current evidence. Sitting administrations are re-reviewed on a rolling basis — at least twice a year, and after major events such as a budget, an election, or a significant court ruling. Former administrations stay frozen at their final review unless new evidence emerges.

Corrections & re-rating

If you think an entry is wrong, outdated, or missing context, email mubaraqsanusi908@gmail.com — corrections are made promptly and noted on the entry. If this methodology itself changes in a way that affects existing ratings, those ratings are re-checked against the new standard within one review cycle, and the change is recorded in the change log below.

7 Independence

A tracker that grades politicians is only worth reading if the grades aren't for sale.

Who decides ratings
Ratings are set by the project's editors against the published methodology (sections 1–2). No subject, source, or sponsor sees or approves a rating before it goes live.
Funding
NGScorecard takes no money from any government, political party, candidate, or campaign — in Nigeria or anywhere else. Any revenue the project does earn (for example paid API access) is kept separate from the rating process, and any sponsorship arrangement is disclosed here.
Ownership
An independent project — not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated on behalf of any government body or political organisation.
Corrections over reputation
When an entry is wrong it is changed and logged (section 8), not defended. Every claim links to its source (section 6) so you can check the work rather than take it on trust.
