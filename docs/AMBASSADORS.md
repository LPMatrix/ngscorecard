# NGScorecard Ambassador Programme

**Status:** Aspirational. This is the operating model for when funding is not the constraint. Nothing here is committed, and nothing in it should be hired for until the runway rules in [HIRING.md](HIRING.md) are met.

---

## The Idea

One ambassador per geopolitical zone, six in total. Each ambassador is NGScorecard's face and ears in their zone. They run events, lead sensitization, and carry the scorecard to people who will never find it online.

The research desk produces the record. Ambassadors make it reach people and bring back what they hear.

## What an Ambassador Does

### 1. Events
- Town halls and community screenings where people look up their own governor's promises against delivery.
- Campus sessions with universities, polytechnics and NYSC orientation camps.
- Election-season forums where candidates' past records are shown beside their new promises.
- Discussion events on youth participation in governance and wider civic topics (see below).

#### Civic discussion events

Ambassadors can convene open discussions that go beyond the scorecard itself, for example:

- Youth participation in governance: how to engage local government, budget hearings, ward development committees and legislators.
- Voter registration, turnout and how to follow an election after the vote.
- Reading a state budget, and tracking a constituency project in your area.
- Civic duties and rights, and how to request public information.

Ground rules, to keep these consistent with the non-partisan line:

- Open to all, with balanced panels or a neutral moderator. No candidate or party gets the platform to themselves.
- Anchor each session in the zone's records from the scorecard where it applies, and cite the source.
- Send a short write-up and attendee questions back to the research desk.
- Treat sponsorship and co-hosting by parties, candidates or officials as a decision for the Communications Lead, not the ambassador.

### 2. Sensitization
- Teach people how to read the scorecard: what "kept", "stalled" and "broken" mean, and what counts as a source.
- Explain how to submit a correction or a tip.
- Work in the zone's languages (Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, Pidgin) in line with the site's i18n plan.

### 3. Dissemination
- Share verified findings with local radio, community leaders, faith and trade groups, market associations and student unions.
- Hand over printed or WhatsApp-ready summaries for places with poor connectivity.
- Build a media contact list for the zone's journalists.

### 4. Listening
- Report back which promises and projects people in the zone are actually asking about.
- Pass on local tips and correction requests to the research desk. They do not verify or publish them.

## What an Ambassador Does Not Do

These limits protect the one thing the organisation cannot afford to lose, which is credibility.

- **No authoring or editing of data.** Findings come only from the research desk and its sources. Ambassadors disseminate; they do not change entries.
- **No partisanship.** No party membership, no party office, no campaign work, no endorsing candidates. Declared at appointment and re-declared annually.
- **No unverified claims in public.** If an ambassador cannot point to the entry and its source, they say "I'll check and come back to you."
- **No fundraising in their own name.** Donations go through the organisation, never to an individual.
- **No dealing with the officials the scorecard covers** in exchange for favours, access or payment.

## Who to Appoint

- Lives in the zone and knows its politics, languages and trust networks.
- Has run community events, a campus group, a civic organisation or a newsroom beat before.
- Has a record of non-partisan work that can be checked.
- Comfortable saying "I don't know yet" in front of a crowd.
- Can handle hostile questions without turning combative.

Check references with the same care as any hire (see HIRING.md).

## How It Fits the Team

```
Founder / Executive
 ├── Research Desk        (verifies, publishes: the only source of findings)
 ├── Operations / Grants  (budget, compliance, reporting)
 └── Communications Lead  (brand, social, national media)
       └── 6 Zonal Ambassadors   (events, sensitization, local dissemination)
```

Ambassadors report to the Communications Lead. The research desk is their only route for corrections and tips. Brand and social media conduct follows the Civic Playbook Ledger guidelines.

## Funding Model (When Money Is Not a Problem)

Cost lines to budget per zone, with amounts still to be set:

- Ambassador stipend or salary
- Event costs (venue, sound, refreshments, printing)
- Transport and security for travel, especially in the North East and North West
- Data and airtime for the ambassador and any volunteers
- Translation and local-language print runs

Funding for the programme is disclosed publicly, consistent with the NGO posture. A funder never directs which governor or administration is highlighted in a zone.

## Measuring Success

Track outcomes, not activity counts.

- People who looked up their own state's record at or after an event (via a tagged link or QR code per event)
- Correction requests and tips received from the zone, and how many led to an update
- Local media pickups of scorecard findings
- Repeat events and partners (schools, unions, community groups)
- Share of zone traffic arriving from ambassador channels

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Ambassador seen as partisan | Annual declaration, non-partisan vetting, immediate suspension on breach |
| Safety in conflict-affected areas | Local security briefings, no solo travel to flagged areas, remote-first events where needed |
| Off-script claims at events | Standard briefing pack, "check and come back" rule |
| Pressure or intimidation from officials | Incident log, legal support, public disclosure policy |
| Funder influence over zonal messaging | Public funding disclosure, written editorial independence clause |
| Ambassador burnout | Part-time options, shared event load with volunteers |
