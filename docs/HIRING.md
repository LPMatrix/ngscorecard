# NGScorecard Hiring Guide

A sequenced guide to building the team as NGScorecard grows. Update this as your needs change.

---

## First Hire: Priority Order

Your first hire should free up the most founder time on a non-core task.

### Option A: Operations / Grant Administrator

**When to hire this first:**
- You spend >10 hours/week on compliance, filings, donor reporting, board setup.
- You have early funding conversations and need clean books.
- Runway is stable (12+ months).

**What they do:**
- NGO registration, tax compliance (annual returns, donor receipts).
- Budget tracking, expense approval, financial reporting to funders.
- Grant deadline tracking, funder reporting.
- Payroll, contracts, board admin.

**What to look for:**
- Has done nonprofit admin or grants before.
- Detail-oriented; can't stand loose ends.
- Comfortable with Google Sheets, basic accounting (Wave, Zoho).
- Proactive about flagging issues early.

**Compensation:** ₦80k–150k/month. Start part-time if cash is tight.

---

### Option B: Fundraiser / Development Lead

**When to hire this first:**
- You have <6 months of runway left.
- You've landed one grant but don't know how to replicate it.
- You have 3+ foundation leads warm but can't chase them all.

**What they do:**
- Foundation research and grant prospecting.
- Grant writing and submission (2–4/month).
- Funder relationship management (calls, updates, renewals).
- Board engagement in fundraising.

**What to look for:**
- Has closed grants before (track record: "I brought in ₦XM for Y organization").
- Comfortable with rejection and persistence.
- Understands your mission deeply.
- Can customize proposals, not template-blast.

**Compensation:** ₦120k–200k/month + optional commission on closes.

---

### Option C: Research / Data Person

**When to hire this first:**
- You have stable funding (12+ months runway).
- The dataset is growing faster than you can verify.
- You are bottlenecked on turning findings into posts.

**What they do:**
- Source and verify promises, fraud cases, budget data.
- Fact-check against seed data and primary sources.
- Turn raw research into desk-note briefs.
- Maintain source documentation and audit trails.

**What to look for:**
- Journalism or research background.
- Can trace a claim to primary sources without hand-holding.
- Comfortable saying "I can't verify this yet."
- Detail-oriented on citations and dates.

**Compensation:** ₦100k–180k/month depending on seniority.

---

## What to Look For in Every Hire

1. **Belief in the mission.** Not activists only, but people who believe in government accountability. Someone chasing a paycheck will leave when things get hard.

2. **Rigor.** Every role requires verifying facts, not guessing. If they hand-wave on details in the interview, they will on the job.

3. **Ownership mentality.** They ask "what if this breaks?" and "how do we measure success?" You want owners, not contractors.

4. **Comfort with ambiguity.** NGO budgets shift, grants fall through, timelines move. Hire people who adapt, not people who need perfect plans.

---

## Common Hiring Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Hiring someone "good at everything"**  
Define the role tightly. "Operations" is a full-time job alone. Don't create burnout roles.

**Mistake 2: Hiring for optimism instead of proof**  
"I've read about grants" ≠ "I closed a ₦10M grant last year." Always ask for a specific win with details.

**Mistake 3: Hiring too early**  
If you hire before 12 months runway, you'll spend all your time fundraising to cover payroll. Use contractors to test roles first.

**Mistake 4: Hiring for your weakness instead of your strength**  
If you're good at research but bad at fundraising, hiring another researcher doesn't solve the problem. Hire for what you can't do.

**Mistake 5: Skipping the reference check**  
NGO trust is your currency. Check with previous funders and employers. Ask about specific wins, not generic praise.

## Interview Checklist

**Every Candidate, Every Role**

- [ ] Specific example of a win (grant closed, compliance audit passed, story published).
- [ ] Why they care about this mission, not just the job.
- [ ] How they handle ambiguity and setbacks.
- [ ] 2+ reference calls with former managers/colleagues.
- [ ] Realistic understanding of the role (not overselling or underselling).

**For Operations Hire:**

- [ ] Example of a funder relationship they managed end-to-end.
- [ ] How they stay organized with multiple deadlines.
- [ ] Comfort with saying "no" (we don't have budget for X).

**For Fundraiser Hire:**

- [ ] Specific grant they closed: amount, funder, timeline, their exact role.
- [ ] How they research foundations and customize proposals.
- [ ] How they handle rejection and pivot.

**For Researcher Hire:**

- [ ] Example of a story they sourced from primary documents.
- [ ] How they verify information and flag uncertainty.
- [ ] Comfort saying "we don't know this yet."

- **Researcher:** ₦100k–180k/month
- **Communications Lead:** ₦80k–150k/month (hire later)

**Benefits to offer:**
- Health insurance (critical for retention)
- 15+ days annual leave
- Professional development budget
- Remote work options (if feasible)

## Onboarding (First 3 Months)

**Week 1–2:**
- Systems access (email, docs, tools).
- Read the Playbook (full for everyone, §11 for researchers).
- 1-on-1 with founder on mission, current challenges, success metrics.

**Week 2–4:**
- Job shadowing or buddy assignment.
- First independent task (small, low-risk).
- Weekly 1-on-1s to catch blockers.

**Month 1–3:**
- Monthly check-in on progress to success metrics.
- Feedback on role fit.
- Decision point: renew, adjust scope, or part ways.

---

## When to Let Someone Go

**Clear red flags:**
- Repeated factual errors (especially researchers; credibility is everything).
- Missed critical deadlines (grant deadline, tax filing) without warning.
- Relationship damage with a funder or key partner.
- Misrepresentation of qualifications during hiring.

**What to do:**
- 30-day improvement plan if performance is fixable and they're coachable.
- Clear conversation: "This role isn't working. Here's why. Here's what success looks like. We have 30 days."
- Separate cleanly and professionally if they're not a fit.

**What not to do:**
- Keep them "just long enough to train a replacement" — erodes team trust.
- Tell them they're underperforming but never give a clear plan to improve.
- Ignore warning signs for months, then fire them suddenly.

## Before You Hire: Founder Checklist

- [ ] You have 12+ months of runway (including new salary).
- [ ] The role is tightly defined (not "do everything").
- [ ] You have a clear success metric (not just "help out").
- [ ] You've interviewed 5+ candidates and checked references.
- [ ] You have a 30-day onboarding plan ready.
- [ ] You're ready to fire them if it's not working (after 30 days of feedback).

---

## Questions to Ask Yourself

1. What am I not doing because I'm too busy? *(That's your hire.)*
2. Can this person scale the work, or just share the load? *(Both valid; know which.)*
3. Do I trust them with donor relationships? *(If no, keep them away from funder-facing work.)*
4. Can I afford to keep them if this fundraising round falls through? *(If not, wait.)*
5. Will they still be here in 2 years? *(Look for long-term commitment signals.)*
