# Youth participation series: plan and posts

Status: draft for review. Nothing here is published. Every post has at least one fact to check before it goes out, marked in the "Check first" line.

## Goal

Give young people concrete ways to take part in the 2027 elections and in governance between elections. Each post should be something a reader can check or do.

## Principles

- **Non-partisan.** No endorsement, no comparison of candidates, no predictions. Posts cover process and records, not individual candidates.
- **Verified before posted.** Every date and procedure is checked against INEC or the official source on the day of publishing.
- **Not about us.** The posts are about the process and the public, not NGScorecard's own work.
- **Nothing to fill a slot.** If a week has no verifiable angle, skip it.

## Key dates (confirm on INEC's site before posting)

- **16 January 2027:** presidential and National Assembly elections.
- **6 February 2027:** governorship and state assembly elections.
- **Voter registration:** the CVR portal shows the final phase closing on 26 July 2026. Some outlets report 30 August, so the current position needs checking.
- **Campaign period and card collection:** not confirmed.

## Cadence and format

- Two posts a week, in the Civic Ledger gallery. Process posts use full Memphis weight. Any post naming an official uses the restrained weight.
- Captions are the final tweet, with no em dashes.
- Pidgin and Yoruba versions follow after the English posts are verified.
- Link posts to the ambassadors' discussion events (docs/AMBASSADORS.md) from phase 2.

## Roles

- Draft and verify: the content lead.
- Second reader: checks each post against its source.
- Counsel: reviews any post that names a person.
- Ambassadors: localise the content.

## Open checks before phase 1

- Whether any registration phase opens after the 26 July 2026 close.
- The campaign calendar and card collection dates.
- The 2027 budget timetable.
- Whether the bill to lower the governor and senate age to 30 (HB 2235) has moved since November 2025.
- The local budget hearing procedure.

## Phase 2: Where the money is decided (16 November to 31 December 2026)

### Post 05: Where the money is decided

- **Window:** 16 Nov to 31 Dec
- **Caption (194 characters):**

  > Nigeria's budget is a law, not a press release. The President lays the proposal before the National Assembly, which passes it. Once it is laid, groups like BudgIT analyse it before it is passed.

- **Checked 16 October 2026:** the process (President lays the estimates, the National Assembly passes them) is confirmed. The 2027 bill had not reached the National Assembly by mid-October; September missed. Re-check the date before posting.

### Post 06: Budget hearings

- **Window:** 16 Nov to 31 Dec
- **Caption (203 characters):**

  > The National Assembly holds public hearings on the budget before it is passed, and civil society groups send memos. Find out whether your state or local government holds hearings, and how to send a view.

- **Card:** Eyebrow: Public Money. Headline: Be heard before the budget passes. Sub: The National Assembly holds public hearings. Find out what your state and LGA offer. Docket: REC. NGSC · PROCESS · BUDGET HEARINGS / SOURCED & LINKED
- **Checked 16 October 2026:** National Assembly public hearings on the 2026 budget are confirmed. Whether individuals can send written views, and how state and LGA hearings work, is not confirmed. Check each state before posting.

### Post 07: Your ward development committee

- **Window:** 16 Nov to 31 Dec
- **Caption (207 characters):**

  > Your ward is where local representation starts. In some states, a ward development committee with elected members from each zone helps set local priorities. Find out whether yours exists, and who sits on it.

- **Card:** Eyebrow: Your Ward. Headline: Where local priorities start. Sub: Does your ward have a development committee, and who sits on it? Docket: REC. NGSC · PROCESS · WARD COMMITTEES / SOURCED & LINKED
- **Checked 16 October 2026:** ward committees exist in some states (one journal study of Ogun State). A ward is not a separate tier of government, so the post must not call it one. Confirm the law in your state before posting.

### Post 08: Your councillor

- **Window:** 16 Nov to 31 Dec
- **Caption (155 characters):**

  > Your councillor represents your ward on the local government council, and takes its needs into the council budget. Know who that is, and how to reach them.

- **Card:** Eyebrow: Your Ward. Headline: Know your councillor. Sub: They represent your ward on the local council. Know who, and how to reach them. Docket: REC. NGSC · PROCESS · LOCAL COUNCILS / SOURCED & LINKED
- **Checked 16 October 2026:** each ward elects a councillor who sits on the LGA council, and councillors take part in allocating council money through the budget. How to reach a councillor varies by state, so the post shouldn't give contact details.
