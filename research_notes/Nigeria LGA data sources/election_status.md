# Nigeria LGA Election Status 2025–2026

## Key Question 1: Which states held LGA elections in 2024 or 2025 and have elected councils?

### Takeaway
Following the Supreme Court's July 2024 ruling on LGA financial autonomy, a wave of states held local government elections in late 2024 and into 2025. By late 2025, at least 30 of the 36 states appear to have elected councils in place, though some elections were held under contested conditions or have uncertain validity.

### Cited Findings

**States with confirmed elected councils (2024–2025):**

- **Lagos** — held elections July 12, 2025; APC won all 57 chairmanship and 375 of 376 councillorship seats. Turnout was reported as low. — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/politics/1664145-breaking-apc-sweeps-lagos-lg-elections-details-emerge); [VON](https://von.gov.ng/2025-lg-elections-lagos-records-peaceful-conduct-low-turnout/)
- **Niger** — held elections November 1, 2025; APC won all 25 chairmanship seats and 271 of 274 councillorship positions. — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/politics/805-candidates-10-parties-for-niger-lg-polls-in-november/); [Prime Business Africa](https://www.primebusiness.africa/apc-tightens-grip-on-niger-politics-with-landslide-lg-poll-victory)

- **Ondo** — held elections January 18, 2025, after caretaker committees were dissolved. Governor had previously constituted caretakers for 18 LGAs and 33 LCDAs in July 2024. — [ICIR Nigeria](https://www.icirnigeria.org/nigerian-states-race-to-conduct-local-government-poll-after-landmark-ruling-on-autonomy/)

- Oyo's exact 2024 election date was not found; the next election is scheduled for 2027.
- Nasarawa's 2024 election result (APC winning all 13 seats) was reported but a precise date was not confirmed.

## Key Question 2: Which states are currently running caretaker/transition committees?

### Takeaway
As of late 2025, only a small number of states appear to still be operating with appointed caretaker structures rather than elected councils, most notably Zamfara, Bayelsa, and (situationally) Ekiti. The pre-ruling landscape saw at least 20 states using caretakers; most have since held elections.

### Cited Findings

- **Zamfara** — last LGA elections held April 2019. The state assembly approved a six-month extension for the caretaker committee in May 2024. No post-ruling elections were found in reporting. — [The Frontier Nigeria](https://thefrontierng.com/20-states-with-local-government-caretaker-committees-full-list/); [ICIR Nigeria](https://www.icirnigeria.org/nigerian-states-race-to-conduct-local-government-poll-after-landmark-ruling-on-autonomy/)
- **Bayelsa** — A June 2024 Situation Room breakdown classified Bayelsa as having no elected LG chairman and described this as illegal. No election was found in 2024 or 2025 reporting. — [Situation Room breakdown (PDF)](https://situationroomng.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/BREAKDOWN-OF-STATES-AND-THEIR-EXISTING-LOCAL-GOVERNMENT-STRUCTURES.pdf); [21st Century Chronicle](https://21stcenturychronicle.com/pdp-appoints-caretaker-committees-for-bayelsa-edo/)
- **Edo** — Reported in August 2025 that the state government and NLC were in a standoff over a planned inauguration of a new caretaker committee, suggesting Edo may have still been using caretakers at that time. — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202508280061.html)
- **Rivers (pre-election)** — Governor Fubara appointed caretaker committees for 23 LGAs amid his political dispute with ex-governor Wike, and inaugurated 23 new LG caretaker chairmen. Later replaced by administrators from April 7, 2025, ahead of the August 2025 elections. — [Gazettengr](https://gazettengr.com/fubara-inaugurates-23-new-lg-caretaker-chairmen-orders-audit-of-outgone-chairs-accounts/); [Prime Business Africa](https://www.primebusiness.africa/rivers-sole-administrator-orders-lg-heads-to-submit-council-reports-amid-alleged-move-to-appoint-caretakers/)

**States that previously used caretakers and have since held elections (as of late 2025):**
The following states were listed as having caretaker committees as of July 2024, but appear to have held elections since:
- Anambra (June 2024 transition committees → elections 2024)
- Bauchi (caretaker → elected August 2024)
- Plateau (transition committees → elected October 2024)
- Abia (transition chairpersons → elected November 2024)
- Enugu (handed over to personnel management March 2024 → elections October 2024)
- Kano (Interim Management Committee March 2024 → elections October 2024)
- Ondo (caretaker committees July 2024 → elected January 2025)
- Osun (extended caretakers to August 2024 → elections February 2025)
- Akwa Ibom (interim administrations approved → elections October 2024)
- Kwara (caretakers since 2020 → elected September 2024)
- Jigawa (dissolved elected chairmen June 2024 → new elections October 2024)
- Yobe (all 17 councils dissolved April 2024) — unclear whether elections followed or if caretakers continue
— [The Frontier Nigeria](https://thefrontierng.com/20-states-with-local-government-caretaker-committees-full-list/)

### Inferences
- Zamfara and Bayelsa are the most likely states to still have appointed caretakers in late 2025, based on available evidence.
- Edo's caretaker standoff in August 2025 is a significant red flag; the governor appeared to be attempting to install new caretakers despite the Supreme Court ruling.
- Yobe's status is unclear: councils were dissolved in April 2024, but whether elections were held afterward was not confirmed.

### Gaps
- Yobe's post-April 2024 status (elected councils or continued caretakers) could not be confirmed.
- Ekiti's LGA election status was not found — only its 2026 governorship election was documented.
- Bayelsa and Zamfara current status as of Nov 2025–Jan 2026 would require checking state government announcements or state SIEC websites.

## Key Question 3: Has the Supreme Court's July 2024 ruling changed the situation?

### Takeaway
The Supreme Court's July 11, 2024 ruling (SC/CV/343/2024) — which barred governors from using caretaker committees and required FAAC allocations go directly to elected councils — triggered a near-universal wave of LGA elections across Nigeria. However, implementation has been uneven, with some states pushing back through legal, legislative, or administrative means.

### Cited Findings

- The Supreme Court on July 11, 2024 ruled that democratically elected Local Government Councils have the constitutional right to receive FAAC allocations directly, and that governors cannot substitute caretaker committees for elected councils. — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2024/07/11/supreme-court-grants-full-financial-autonomy-to-774-local-government-areas)
- The ruling explicitly barred the 36 states from interrupting LGA elections or dissolving elected councils. — [Channels TV](https://www.channelstv.com/2024/07/12/lg-autonomy-ctc-chairmen-will-not-get-money-from-faac-ozekhome)
- Post-ruling, at least 13 states immediately announced election dates, and no fewer than 10 held elections within months. — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/supreme-court-ruling-13-states-rush-to-hold-local-government-elections/); [ICIR Nigeria](https://www.icirnigeria.org/nigerian-states-race-to-conduct-local-government-poll-after-landmark-ruling-on-autonomy/)
- The ALGON body accused state finance commissioners of colluding with governors to frustrate the order, suggesting the FAAC enforcement mechanism was not immediately applied uniformly. — [African Cities](https://www.african-cities.org/?p=8584)
- Lagos passed an Autonomy Law after the ruling but also converted its 37 LCDAs into Area Administrative Councils headed by appointed secretaries, potentially circumventing the spirit of the ruling. — [African Cities](https://www.african-cities.org/is-lagos-moving-in-circles-on-local-government-autonomy/)
- Anambra's 2024 local government law was criticized for including provisions that undermine the judgment, limiting administrative autonomy even while the financial side was addressed. — [African Cities](https://www.african-cities.org/?p=8584)
- Oyo's governor initially said FAAC would delay implementation by 90 days. — [African Cities](https://www.african-cities.org/?p=8584)
- In Rivers State, the Supreme Court annulled the October 5, 2024 RSIEC election (which APP won 22/23 seats) — demonstrating the courts continued to play an active role in LGA governance post-ruling. — [ICIR Nigeria](https://www.icirnigeria.org/apc-wins-20-out-of-23-lgas-in-rivers-local-government-poll/)

### Inferences
- The financial consequence (losing FAAC allocations) was the most powerful enforcement lever, and it largely worked: states that wanted federal funds rushed to hold elections.
- However, some states found workarounds (Lagos LCDA appointed secretaries, Anambra's legislative provisions) that preserve some gubernatorial control over LGA governance while technically complying with the electoral requirement.
- The state-of-emergency declaration in Rivers State and the subsequent annulment of elections show that the crisis around LGA governance is ongoing in at least one state.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed how consistently the FAAC direct-payment requirement was enforced across states with caretakers still in place (Zamfara, Bayelsa, possibly others).
- Detailed analysis of which states received FAAC directly to elected councils vs. those where funds were still routed through state governments was not found.

## Key Question 5: States with mixed, uncertain, or irregular situations

### Takeaway
Several states present genuinely complex or contested LGA governance situations as of late 2025, where the legal status of elected councils is disputed or unclear.

### Cited Findings

- **Rivers State** — Most complex case. Timeline: (1) Governor Fubara appointed caretaker committees for all 23 LGAs in 2024 amid a political crisis with ex-governor Wike; (2) An October 5, 2024 RSIEC election saw APP win 22/23 seats — results that were later annulled by the Supreme Court; (3) A state of emergency was declared; (4) Administrators were named from April 7, 2025; (5) New elections held August 30–31, 2025, with APC winning 20/23 seats, PDP winning 3. The opposition IPAC rejected the August 2025 poll as illegal under the state of emergency. — [Prime Business Africa](https://www.primebusiness.africa/rivers-sole-administrator-orders-lg-heads-to-submit-council-reports-amid-alleged-move-to-appoint-caretakers/); [ICIR Nigeria](https://www.icirnigeria.org/apc-wins-20-out-of-23-lgas-in-rivers-local-government-poll/); [The Cable](https://www.thecable.ng/breaking-apc-wins-20-lgas-pdp-secures-three-in-rivers-council-polls)
- **Edo** — As of August 2025, the Edo State government and NLC were in a standoff over the inauguration of caretaker committee members, suggesting the state was attempting to install appointed officials in contravention of the Supreme Court ruling. — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202508280061.html)
- **Lagos** — While Lagos held elections (July 2025), it also converted its 37 LCDAs into Area Administrative Councils headed by appointed secretaries rather than elected officials. This is a potential evasion of the ruling's intent in the LCDA (sub-LGA) units. — [African Cities](https://www.african-cities.org/is-lagos-moving-in-circles-on-local-government-autonomy/)
- **Anambra** — Held elections in 2024, but the state's new Local Government Law contains provisions criticized for undermining the Supreme Court ruling, limiting administrative autonomy even as financial autonomy nominally exists. — [African Cities](https://www.african-cities.org/?p=8584)
- **Zamfara** — The state had been running caretaker committees since 2019. A six-month extension was approved in May 2024 by the state assembly. No election was found in searches, making it a possible ongoing non-compliance case. — [The Frontier Nigeria](https://thefrontierng.com/20-states-with-local-government-caretaker-committees-full-list/)
- **Bayelsa** — Classified as having no elected LG chairman in a June 2024 civic analysis. No post-ruling election was found in any source. — [Situation Room Nigeria breakdown PDF](https://situationroomng.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/BREAKDOWN-OF-STATES-AND-THEIR-EXISTING-LOCAL-GOVERNMENT-STRUCTURES.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Rivers situation is the most legally contested: multiple elections, a state of emergency, Supreme Court intervention, and opposition claims that all elections under emergency are void.
- Edo's August 2025 caretaker standoff and Zamfara's extended caretaker structure suggest that at least two states may be in active non-compliance with the July 2024 Supreme Court ruling as of late 2025.
- Lagos's LCDA appointed secretaries is a structural workaround that may face future legal challenge.

### Gaps
- Whether Zamfara or Bayelsa ultimately held post-ruling elections could not be confirmed from available sources.
- Ekiti State's LGA election status (distinct from the 2026 governorship election) was not found in any source.
- Yobe State's status after the April 2024 dissolution of all 17 councils could not be confirmed.
- The actual FAAC payment trail (which states received direct payments, which did not) could not be determined from press reports alone.
