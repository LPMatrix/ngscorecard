# Three Data Gaps Block Nigeria's LGA Scorecard

Nigeria's 774 LGAs can be enriched with 2006 census population figures, federal constituency mappings, and council election status, but none of the three data layers has a single authoritative, freely licensed, machine-readable download ready to use. The best available path for each layer is workable but requires some manual effort: a PDF extraction or cautious SQL import, a cross-check against Wikipedia, and a state-by-state news reconciliation. The 2024 Supreme Court ruling on LGA autonomy has transformed the election-status picture almost entirely in the past 18 months, meaning this layer is both the most politically significant and the most rapidly changing. A civic accountability website can get all three layers populated, but must accept that data quality varies by layer, licences are not all confirmed, and a handful of states remain genuinely contested or unresolved.

## Census population exists in PDFs, not in a clean download

The **official 2006 census national total is 140,431,790**, published in the Nigerian Government Gazette dated 2 February 2009 ([Government Gazette](https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/ng/2009/ng-government-gazette-dated-2009-02-02-no-2.pdf)). The National Population Commission (NPC) conducted the census from 21–27 March 2006, and the LGA-level breakdowns appear across four volumes of Priority Tables accessible through the IHSN Data Catalog ([IHSN catalog](https://datacatalog.ihsn.org/catalog/3340/related-materials)). **Priority Table Volume III** (Population Distribution by Sex, State, LGA and Senatorial District, 7.05 MB) is the single most useful file: it has all LGAs in one place. The IHSN access policy requires citation but names no Creative Commons licence — a real but manageable constraint for a non-commercial civic site that attributes NPC explicitly.

The best candidate for a ready-to-use CSV is the Open Africa listing "2006 Census Population Distribution by L.G.A" (resource ID `51aa18bb-ab54-47dd-a144-494f41496bc8`), attributed to the NPC and originally published via Edo State's open-data portal ([Open Africa](https://open.africa/dataset/2006-census-population-distribution-by-l-g-a1/resource/51aa18bb-ab54-47dd-a144-494f41496bc8)). It returned HTTP 403 during research, so its national coverage and exact column structure are unconfirmed; it may cover only Edo State. HDX also hosts two relevant datasets — "Nigeria - Subnational Population Statistics" (`cod-ps-nga`) and a combined boundaries-and-tables file ([HDX](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/cod-ps-nga)) — both of which returned 403 errors but are believed, from prior knowledge of HDX Common Operational Datasets, to carry CC BY licences; if confirmed, they would be the strongest choice on both coverage and licence clarity. GitHub repositories such as `temikeezy/nigeria-geojson-data` ([GitHub](https://github.com/temikeezy/nigeria-geojson-data)) offer LGA boundary data but no population figures; `Igbah/nigeria-explorer` ([GitHub](https://github.com/Igbah/nigeria-explorer)) has LGA population but draws on 2025 GRID3/WorldPop satellite estimates, not the 2006 census.

Two data quality caveats apply to any 2006 dataset. First, CityPopulation.de explicitly notes "high error rates; census results are disputed" ([CityPopulation](https://citypopulation.de/en/nigeria/admin/)) — the Lagos vs. Kano population ranking provoked the most public controversy, with Lagos State authorities disputing Kano's figure of 9,401,288 against Lagos's 9,113,605 ([Wikipedia Demographics of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Nigeria)). Second, the NPC counts **775 LGAs** (matching the 1999 Constitution), while most datasets and references cite 774; the discrepancy traces to a "Disputed Areas" entry that some compilers omit. Any implementation should validate by summing LGA figures per state against the known state totals, and summing all LGAs to check against 140,431,790. Sample anchor points for cross-checking: **Alimosho (Lagos) = 1,319,571** (most populous LGA) and **Bakassi = 31,641** (least populous) appear consistently across secondary sources ([Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/ask-legit/top/1130107-largest-local-government-area-nigeria-by-population-top-10/)).

## Constituency mapping: a SQL dump beats scattered PDFs

**360 federal constituencies cover 774 LGAs** at a mean ratio of about 2.17 LGAs per constituency, making the mapping a true many-to-many table: most constituencies group two to four LGAs, while at least six LGAs in Lagos, Rivers, and Oyo are each split across two constituencies ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_federal_constituencies)). Lagos illustrates the extremes — its 24 constituencies cover 20 LGAs, requiring at least four LGAs to be divided. Confirmed split cases include Lagos Island I/II, Mushin I/II, Surulere I/II, and Oshodi-Isolo I/II.

The constitutional authority for constituency delimitation rests with INEC under Section 71(b) of the 1999 Constitution, with Section 73(1) mandating review at least every ten years ([Street Lawyer Naija](https://streetlawyernaija.com/?p=7748)). In practice, **no comprehensive review has occurred since 1999**; the House of Representatives noted that INEC has not complied with Section 73(1) ([NAN News](https://nannews.ng/reps-task-inec-on-constituency-delineation/)). The sole exception is the Warri Federal Constituency, where a Supreme Court judgment (*Timinimi & Others v INEC*, SC.413/2016) forced INEC to conduct a delineation in Delta State, with a final report released in May 2026 ([The Frontier NG](https://thefrontierng.com/?p=93128)). The practical consequence for a data pipeline is that the 1999 boundary assignments are still the operative ones for 35 of 36 states plus the FCT.

INEC does not publish a national machine-readable crosswalk. The most usable structured source is **GitHub repo `nuhu-ibrahim/lga-districs`** ([GitHub](https://github.com/nuhu-ibrahim/lga-districs)), a MariaDB SQL dump in which `lga_tbl` stores `representative_district_id` as a foreign key into `representative_district_tbl`, which holds all 360 constituency names. A single SQL JOIN produces the complete LGA-to-constituency table. The repo claims "760+ local governments," so roughly a dozen LGAs may be missing; it carries no official INEC attribution. The Wikipedia "List of Nigerian federal constituencies" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_federal_constituencies)) is scrapable and effectively mirrors the current constitutional text. State-level PDFs from situationroomng.org (INEC data prepared for the 2015 elections) provide the same information per state with registration-area counts. The HDX "INEC LGA and Wards" dataset ([HDX](https://data.humdata.org/dataset/nigeria-independent-national-electoral-commission-lga-and-wards)) was inaccessible during research but is worth retrying; if it includes ward detail it may be the richest administrative crosswalk available.

## Election status transformed by a single Supreme Court ruling

Before July 2024, at least 20 of 36 states were running appointed caretaker committees in place of elected councils — in some cases, such as Zamfara (last election April 2019) and Kwara (last election November 2017), for years ([The Frontier Nigeria](https://thefrontierng.com/20-states-with-local-government-caretaker-committees-full-list/)). The Supreme Court ruling of **July 11, 2024** (SC/CV/343/2024) barred governors from using caretaker committees and required FAAC allocations to be paid directly to elected councils, not funnelled through state governments ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2024/07/11/supreme-court-grants-full-financial-autonomy-to-774-local-government-areas)). The threat of losing federal revenue was the decisive enforcement lever: **at least 30 of 36 states held LGA elections within roughly 18 months of the ruling**, most in a concentrated October–November 2024 wave.

State-level data is well documented for most jurisdictions. Lagos held elections on **July 12, 2025** with APC sweeping all 57 chairmanship seats ([Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/politics/1664145-breaking-apc-sweeps-lagos-lg-elections-details-emerge)). Niger State held elections on **November 1, 2025**, APC winning all 25 chairmanship seats ([Prime Business Africa](https://www.primebusiness.africa/apc-tightens-grip-on-niger-politics-with-landslide-lg-poll-victory)). Rivers State — the most legally fraught case — had an October 2024 APP-won election annulled by the Supreme Court, a state of emergency declared, and new APC-dominated elections held on **August 30–31, 2025** ([ICIR Nigeria](https://www.icirnigeria.org/apc-wins-20-out-of-23-lgas-in-rivers-local-government-poll/)). FCT Area Councils, which operate under INEC rather than a SIEC, held elections on **February 21, 2026** ([VON](https://von.gov.ng/?p=384224)).

Three states remain in contested or uncertain status as of late 2025. **Zamfara** had its caretaker mandate extended by six months in May 2024 and no subsequent election was reported ([ICIR Nigeria](https://www.icirnigeria.org/nigerian-states-race-to-conduct-local-government-poll-after-landmark-ruling-on-autonomy/)). **Bayelsa** was classified as having no elected LG chairman in a June 2024 Situation Room analysis ([Situation Room PDF](https://situationroomng.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/BREAKDOWN-OF-STATES-AND-THEIR-EXISTING-LOCAL-GOVERNMENT-STRUCTURES.pdf)) and no post-ruling election was found. **Edo** was in a government-NLC standoff over caretaker inaugurations as late as August 2025 ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202508280061.html)). Yobe and Ekiti also lack confirmed election dates. Beyond outright non-compliance, several states show structural workarounds: Lagos converted its 37 LCDAs into appointed-secretary Area Administrative Councils ([African Cities](https://www.african-cities.org/is-lagos-moving-in-circles-on-local-government-autonomy/)), and Anambra's 2024 local government law drew criticism for limiting administrative autonomy despite financial compliance ([African Cities](https://www.african-cities.org/?p=8584)). The operative tracker for date-level data is ShineYourEye ([ShineYourEye](https://www.shineyoureye.org/info/local-government-elections)).

## Recommended implementation path for the website

For **2006 census population**, the recommended sequence is: (1) retry the Open Africa CSV from a non-Nigerian IP, as 403 errors are often geographic; if that fails, (2) retry the HDX `cod-ps-nga` dataset, which likely carries CC BY and may be cleaner; if both remain inaccessible, (3) extract Priority Table Volume III from the IHSN catalog using `pdftotext` or a PDF parsing library and build the CSV manually, attributing NPC with the IHSN citation format. Validate by summing per-state totals against the published state figures, and reject any dataset whose national total deviates more than 1% from 140,431,790.

For **federal constituency mapping**, import the `nuhu-ibrahim/lga-districs` SQL dump, run the JOIN to produce a flat CSV, then spot-check 30–50 rows against the Wikipedia constituency table. Flag the Warri/Delta rows as requiring post-May 2026 review. Accept that the dataset covers approximately 760 of 774 LGAs and plan a cleanup pass to fill the ~14 gaps using state-level situationroomng.org PDFs. Store the mapping as a many-to-one table (one row per LGA-constituency pair) to handle split LGAs cleanly.

For **election status**, use ShineYourEye as the live primary tracker, supplemented by state SIEC press releases. Encode status as a four-value enum: **elected** (election date confirmed, term not yet expired), **caretaker/appointed** (no confirmed election post–July 2024 ruling), **contested** (election held but legally challenged or status disputed), and **unknown** (Ekiti, Yobe, possibly others). Build in an update cadence — LGA council terms are three years, so the 2024 election cohort expires in 2027, which means a large refresh will be needed in roughly two years. The Rivers and Zamfara/Bayelsa situations should be flagged with editorial notes rather than a simple status badge, since the underlying legal disputes have civic significance that a single status field cannot capture.

## Conclusion

The most reliable and fastest path to a production-ready LGA enrichment is to combine the NPC Priority Table Volume III PDF (for population), the nuhu-ibrahim SQL dump cross-checked against Wikipedia (for constituency mapping), and the ShineYourEye tracker with manual state-SIEC verification for the contested states (for election status). No single source covers all three dimensions, and the licence picture for census data remains technically unresolved — an attribution-only requirement from IHSN is workable in practice but should be disclosed on the site. The election-status layer is the one with ongoing legal and political dynamics: Zamfara, Bayelsa, Edo, and Rivers require monitoring, not just one-time data entry, and the 2027 mass expiry of October 2024 council terms will require another significant data refresh. A civic accountability site built on this foundation should treat election status as a live feed and census population as stable historical context, designing its data model accordingly.
